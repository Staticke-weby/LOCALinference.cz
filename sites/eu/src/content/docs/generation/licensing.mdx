With generative models, the license often matters before quality. A
model that makes the best images may ban commercial use — or exclude the
European Union entirely.

## Three separate questions

1. **Weights license.** What may you do with the model — use, modify,
   redistribute, sell outputs?
2. **Output license.** Most licenses leave outputs to you. Qwen Research
   restricts the model, not the image itself.
3. **Territory.** Some licenses apply only in a defined territory.
   Tencent Hunyuan explicitly excludes the EU, UK and South Korea.

For a company in Czechia, the third question is the most common trap:
the model downloads and runs, but the license does not permit use in
the EU at all.

## What is safe

| License | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Apache-2.0** | Use, modify and commercialise, no fee | Z-Image-Turbo, FLUX.2 klein, VoxCPM2, MADLAD-400 |
| **MIT** | Same, shorter text | TRELLIS.2, Pixal3D, Chatterbox, Ming-3.0 |

## Conditional

| License | Condition | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **LTX-2.x Community** | Free under $10M annual revenue; paid above | LTX-2.5 |
| **Tencent Hunyuan Community** | Excludes EU, UK and South Korea; extra terms above 100M users | HunyuanImage 3.0, HunyuanVideo 1.5, Hunyuan3D 2.0 |

LTX is fine for small and mid-sized companies. Tencent is a fundamental
problem: **in Czechia the model cannot be used**, because the whole EU
is excluded. It is not a "condition", it is missing permission.

## Non-commercial

| License | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Qwen Research License** | Research and evaluation only; commercial use needs a separate license | Qwen-Image 2.1, Qwen-Image-Edit 2.1 |
| **FLUX non-commercial** | Non-commercial weights; commercial license is purchased | FLUX.2 dev |

## Checklist before deployment

- **Who owns the rights** — an individual, a lab, a company?
- **Does the license say "commercial", or only "research"?**
- **Does it exclude a territory?** Look for "Territory" and "European Union".
- **Is there a revenue or user limit?**
- **What does it say about outputs?**
- **Are third-party components under different licenses?**
- **Is this for a product, or internal use?**

## Where it falls short

- **Licenses change** with new versions; every record has a date.
- **Outputs can carry their own rights** — people, trademarks, music.
- **Voice cloning** is restricted by most licenses regardless of the model.
- **This is not legal advice.** For large contracts, ask a lawyer.
