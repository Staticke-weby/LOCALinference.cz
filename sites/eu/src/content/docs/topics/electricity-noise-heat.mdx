**Electricity** is a small item next to the price of the machine, but
with all-day operation do not ignore it. **Noise** decides whether the
machine can sit in an office. **Heat** decides whether it keeps its
performance.

## Short answer

- **Draw depends on the machine:** 45–80 W for a laptop, 120–240 W
  for a box.
- **Yearly consumption follows from watts and hours.**
- **We have no public noise measurements** — so we do not invent dB
  figures.
- **Heat means throttling**, not failure.

## Draw of known machines

| Device | Draw | Note |
|---|---:|---|
| DGX Spark | 140 W chip | 240 W power supply |
| RTX Spark mini | 140 W | desktop |
| RTX Spark notebook | 45–80 W | depends on the mode |
| Gorgon Halo | 120 W | 160 W peak |
| Strix Halo | not stated | vendors differ |
| Mac Studio | max 480 W | power supply ceiling |
| MacBook Pro | not stated | depends on configuration |

These figures come from vendor documentation, not from our
measurements. For Strix Halo and the MacBook there is no public
figure under load.

## Calculating consumption

```
kWh per year = draw (kW) × hours per day × days per year
```

An illustrative **calculation** at 8 hours and 250 days:

| Draw | kWh/year |
|---:|---:|
| 80 W | 160 |
| 120 W | 240 |
| 140 W | 280 |
| 240 W | 480 |

The cost is the number of kWh times your tariff. We do not publish a
price because it changes with the supplier and the plan.

## Example: two boxes in an office

A small company runs two 128GB boxes at 140 W, 8 hours a day, 250
days a year.

Illustrative **calculation**:

```
0.14 kW × 8 h × 250 days × 2 machines = 560 kWh per year
```

That is roughly one extra fridge. The cost is 560 times your tariff.
Laptops at 60 W draw about **240 kWh**, less than half.

The difference between 140 W and 240 W is not hundreds of units of
currency; it is the heat in the room. Two boxes at 240 W already heat
like a small radiator, and in summer you notice.

## Noise

We do not have public noise measurements under load for most of these
machines. So we publish no dB figures. What we do know:

- **A laptop** has a smaller cooler and higher fan speeds.
- **A box** has a larger cooler and tends to be quieter.
- **Mac Studio** is effectively passive; noise is minimal.
- **DGX Spark** has active cooling; the specific dB figure is missing.

Noise is **D12** in our gap register. When a public measurement
appears, we will add it.

## Measuring noise yourself

We have no public dB figures, but you can measure your own machine
with a phone:

1. **Put the machine where it will actually sit**, not in a cupboard.
2. **Phone one metre away**, at ear height.
3. **Measure at idle**, under normal load and after 15 minutes.
4. **Note the room noise** before switching on.
5. **Compare with conversation** — a normal office is around 40 dB.

Phone apps are not calibrated, but for comparing two machines in the
same room they are enough. Do not present the phone number as exact.

## Heat and throttling

- **Watts become heat** — 1 W ≈ 3.41 BTU/h.
- **240 W** is roughly **820 BTU/h**.
- **A small room** warms up; in summer that means a higher
  temperature and throttling.
- **A dusty filter** reduces airflow.

Throttling shows up as a fall in tok/s after a few minutes. It is not
a fault; it is the chip protecting itself.

## What to do

- **Put the machine where air flows**, not in a cupboard.
- **Plan for summer heat.**
- **On a laptop, measure performance on battery and on the charger.**
- **Do not cover the vents.**
- **For batch jobs, plan pauses.**

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it happens | How to avoid it |
|---|---|---|
| Counting only the peak | "it is a 240 W machine" | use the average under load |
| Ignoring idle | the machine runs all day | measure with a wall meter |
| Mixing up prices and tariffs | different plans | use the final price per kWh |
| Underestimating heat | "it will cool itself" | count BTU/h |
| A machine in a cupboard | "so it is quiet" | keep the airflow |

## When not to bother

- A short test.
- A machine in a cool server room.
- When electricity is negligible in the budget.
- When the vendor only states the maximum and you never run at full load.
