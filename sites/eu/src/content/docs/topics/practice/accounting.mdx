Accounting is ideal for local AI: many invoices, repeated tasks and
sensitive data. It is also true that **the number must be right**. The
model can help with the first pass, but validation and human approval
remain.

## Short answer

- **Invoice extraction** is a safe start; **automatic posting** is not.
- **Every amount, date and company ID** is checked against the
  document.
- **A local box** keeps data in the company, but does not solve the
  audit trail.
- **The output must be structured** and validated.

## Where it makes sense

| Task | What the model does | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Invoice extraction** | supplier, company ID, amount, VAT | a wrong digit |
| **Document checks** | missing fields, duplicates | a false match |
| **Report preparation** | a summary from the data | a wrong total |
| **Audit support** | an overview of documents | missing evidence |
| **Client communication** | an e-mail draft | the wrong tone |

## Workflow

1. **Receipt** — PDF, scan, e-mail.
2. **Extraction** — OCR and structured fields.
3. **Validation** — company ID, VAT ID, amounts, VAT, dates.
4. **Check** — a person against the document.
5. **Posting** — only after approval.
6. **Archiving** — the document, the output, the log.

## What to validate

- **Company ID (IČO)** — 8 digits in Czechia, and the register.
- **VAT ID (DIČ)** — prefix and format.
- **Date** — issue, due, and the Czech DUZP (the date of taxable
  supply).
- **Amount** — base, VAT, total.
- **VAT rate** — matching the type of supply.
- **Currency** — the currency and, if converted, the rate used.
- **Duplicates** — the same document twice.

The Czech identifiers are a local example. Germany has a
Umsatzsteuer-IdNr., the UK a VAT registration number, the US an EIN;
the fields are the same, the formats and registers are not.

## Data and audit

- **Documents** have retention periods; do not delete them on your own
  initiative.
- **Logs** must show who changed what.
- **Access** for authorised people only.
- **Backups** and restore.
- **Permissions** for the personal data on the documents.

## Risks

- **A wrong digit** — the model reads 1 000 as 10 000.
- **Company ID swapped for VAT ID** — a different identifier.
- **The wrong VAT rate.**
- **A duplicate payment.**
- **A missing document** in the report.
- **Automation without a check.**

## Example: checking an invoice

An accountant uploads an invoice. The model pulls out the supplier, the
company ID, the amount and the VAT. Validation reveals that the sum
does not add up. The accountant returns the invoice to the supplier.
Without validation the error would have gone into the books. The model
helped, but did not decide.

## What to measure

- **The number of invoices** per hour.
- **The error rate** after validation.
- **How many** documents pass without a correction.
- **The time** spent checking.
- **The number of duplicates** and missing fields.

## How much time it saves

An illustrative **calculation** for 500 invoices a month:

| Phase | By hand | With the model |
|---|---|---|
| Reading and transcription, 500 invoices | 3 min × 500 = 25 h | 40 s × 500 = 5.6 h |
| Check against the document | included | 1 min × 500 = 8.3 h |
| Total | ~25 h | ~13.9 h |

These are a **calculation** with sample inputs, not a measurement of
your company. The point is the ratio: the model shortens the
transcription, not the check. When the check costs as much as the
transcription, the saving is small.

## How to introduce it

1. **Pick one document type** — domestic invoices, not all of them at
  once.
2. **Define the fields** and their validation (company ID, amounts,
   DUZP).
3. **Prepare 50 invoices** including problematic ones (scan, foreign
   currency).
4. **Measure the error rate** before and after the check.
5. **Introduce states** — new, extracted, checked, posted.
6. **Only then** extend to other document types.

Automatic posting without a person is the last step, not the first. Even
when the model works reliably, the company carries the responsibility
for the books.

## Typical mistakes

| Mistake | Why it happens | How to avoid it |
|---|---|---|
| Company ID and VAT ID swapped | similar formats | validate both fields separately |
| The wrong VAT rate | the model does not know the type of supply | a rate table and a check |
| A duplicate payment | the same invoice in two formats | a hash and matching |
| A missing DUZP | the field is not in the schema | a required field and validation |
| A transcribed amount | OCR | check the sum against the document |

## When not to use it

- **Automatic posting** without a person.
- **A tax return** without a professional check.
- **Poor scans** without OCR.
- **Documents** with unreadable amounts.
- **An audit** without the source documents.
