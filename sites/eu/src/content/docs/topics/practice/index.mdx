Local AI is not a product you buy and switch on. It is a tool that has
to fit a **specific profession, specific documents and a specific
responsibility**. This cluster shows four areas where starting makes
sense: law, accounting, manufacturing and software development.

## Short answer

- **Start with one task**, not “AI for the whole company”.
- **Data and responsibility** decide more than the model.
- **A person stays in control** of anything with a legal or financial
  impact.
- **Measure time, errors and quality** — without a baseline the benefit
  cannot be shown.

## The common pattern

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| **1. Task** | pick repeated work with documents |
| **2. Data** | find out where it comes from and who may see it |
| **3. Model** | Qwen3.8 Flash Next on a 128GB box |
| **4. Workflow** | input → model → check → output |
| **5. Risks** | error, leak, liability, licence |
| **6. Measurement** | time, errors, quality, cost |

The pattern holds in all four professions. Only the data, the regulation
and the consequences of an error differ.

## Four professions

- **[Law](/topics/practice/law/)** — contracts, case law, confidentiality.
- **[Accounting](/topics/practice/accounting/)** — invoices, VAT, audit.
- **[Manufacturing](/topics/practice/manufacturing/)** — manuals, documentation, offline.
- **[Developers](/topics/practice/developers/)** — code, tests, agents.

## Which profession to start with

| Profession | Best first task | Consequence of error | Demands on the check |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Law** | summary and clause extraction | high | citations and review |
| **Accounting** | invoice extraction | high | numbers and validation |
| **Manufacturing** | search in a manual | safety | document version |
| **Developers** | tests and review | medium | tests and diff |

Start where there is **fast feedback**: an accountant sees that the sum
does not add up; a developer sees that the test passed. Law and
manufacturing have more expensive verification, so they need a stricter
process.

## What the first month looks like

1. **Week 1** — choose the task, 20–50 samples, a manual baseline.
2. **Week 2** — structured output and validation.
3. **Week 3** — test on the samples, measure the error rate.
4. **Week 4** — a pilot with one user and a collection of errors.

Without the baseline from week 1, week 4 has nothing to compare with.
That is the most common reason why the benefit cannot be defended.

## What they have in common

- **Documents** are both the input and the output.
- **Czech** and professional terminology decide the outcome.
- **The check** is part of the workflow, not an afterthought.
- **Permissions** belong in the application, not in the prompt.
- **Backups** and audit are mandatory for sensitive data.

## Example: law and accounting

A law firm starts with contract summaries, an accounting firm with
invoice extraction. Both have the same foundation: documents,
validation and a human check. Only the consequences of an error and the
regulation differ. That is why law puts more weight on citations and
confidentiality, accounting on numbers and audit.

## When it makes no sense

- The task is **a one-off**.
- The data is **public** and the cloud does not bother you.
- You have **no one to check** the output.
- You cannot **measure the benefit**.
- The project is just **“we want AI”** without a process.

## Where the limit is

- **Regulation** — in law, accounting and HR the interpretation decides,
  not the model.
- **Data** — without permissions and good documents even the best model
  will not help.
- **The check** — someone has to actually read the output; without that
  the risk is higher than the benefit.
- **Measurement** — without a baseline the benefit can neither be
  defended nor improved.
- **Capacity** — one 128GB box serves 1–4 people at once
  ([calculation](/choosing/how-many-users/)).
