Extraction is a task where the model does not write text, it **fills
fields**. From an invoice or a contract it should get the supplier, the
company registration number, the VAT ID, the date, the amount and other
data. The output then goes into a table or a system. This is where
accuracy decides, not fluency.

## Short answer

- **Extraction is not summarising.** The goal is structured output, not
  nice prose.
- **Use a JSON schema and validation**; the model must not invent
  fields.
- **Every value is checked** against the source, numbers and dates in
  particular.
- **A local model suits** sensitive documents, but not as an unattended
  robot.

## What to extract

### Invoice

| Field | Example | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Supplier | company name | diacritics, legal form |
| Company ID (IČO) | 8 digits | confused with the VAT ID |
| VAT ID (DIČ) | CZ + number | missing prefix |
| Issue date | 10 November 2026 | format confusion |
| Due date | 24 November 2026 | off by one day |
| Amount excl. VAT | 10 000 | rounding |
| VAT | 2 100 | rate |
| Total | 12 100 | sum |
| Reference | variable symbol | swapped |

The `variable symbol` is the Czech payment reference that ties a payment
to an invoice. Other countries use a structured reference or a payment
note; keep whichever your bank and accounting system use.

The amounts are illustrative and currency-neutral on purpose. The
fields and the risks are the same whether your invoices are in EUR, USD
or another currency.

### Contract

- the parties and their company IDs,
- the subject of performance,
- the price and payment terms,
- the effective date and termination,
- the notice period,
- penalties and liability,
- the governing law.

## JSON schema

Give the model an exact structure. For example:

```json
{
  "supplier": "string",
  "company_id": "string",
  "vat_id": "string",
  "issue_date": "YYYY-MM-DD",
  "amount_excl_vat": "number",
  "vat": "number",
  "total": "number",
  "currency": "EUR"
}
```

When the model returns a different structure, validation fails. That is
correct. Better to fail than to store a wrong number.

## Procedure

1. **Prepare the document** — text, or a scan with OCR.
2. **Split it** into the header, the line items and the totals.
3. **Send the schema** and concrete instructions.
4. **Validate the JSON** — types, required fields, ranges.
5. **Check against the source** — amounts, dates, company IDs.
6. **Store the uncertainty too** — which field the model did not
   deliver.

## A sample prompt

> From this document, extract the fields according to the JSON schema.
> If a field is missing, return null. Do not invent values. For amounts,
> keep the currency and the decimal separator. For dates, use the
> YYYY-MM-DD format. The output must be valid JSON, without comments.

Attach the schema as part of the prompt or as a tool. The model then
does not have to guess the structure.

## Checking the company ID and VAT

- **Company ID** — verify it against the public register, not just the
  length.
- **VAT ID** — check it including the country prefix.
- **VAT** — calculate it from the base and the rate; do not rely on the
  model.
- **The sum** of the line items must match the total.
- **The date** — compare it with the text, not with today.

## Validation

- **Company ID** — 8 digits in Czechia; verify against the register,
  not just a regex. Other countries use a tax number or a company
  number of a different length.
- **VAT ID** — the Czech form is the prefix CZ and a number; foreign
  ones have other formats.
- **Date** — one format; watch 01/02 vs. 1 February.
- **Amount** — the sum of the items must add up.
- **VAT** — the rate and the calculation must agree.
- **Currency** — keep whatever the invoice uses; do not convert without
  an exchange rate.

## Czech specifics

- **Diacritics** — company names and people's names must stay exact.
- **Legal form** — s.r.o., a.s., v.o.s.; do not abbreviate on your own
  initiative.
- **Company ID vs. VAT ID** — they are not the same; the model can swap
  them.
- **Amounts** — Czech formatting uses a space for thousands and a comma
  for decimals (`10 000,50`).
- **Dates** — Czech order is day, month, year.

## How many invoices a box handles

Extraction is a short answer, so decode is the main brake. An
illustrative **calculation**: a 400-token output at 50 tok/s is 8
seconds per invoice. A batch of 200 invoices then comes to **~27
minutes** of pure generation.

On top of that you need OCR for scans and a human check. If the check
takes a minute per invoice, that is 200 minutes — **five times more
than the model itself**. That is why you optimise the check
(validation, matching, a queue for suspicious cases only), not the
model.

The numbers are a **calculation** with sample inputs, not a
measurement. With four users on one box, the per-user speed drops to
roughly a quarter (see
[capacity](/choosing/how-many-users/)).

## When vision instead of OCR

| Situation | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A stack of scans, printed text | OCR | cheap and accurate |
| A photo of an invoice from a phone | vision model | handles perspective |
| A table with many columns | OCR with layout | keeps the structure |
| Handwritten notes | vision + a person | OCR fails |
| One document a day | vision | a pipeline is not worth it |

A vision model is more convenient, but at large volumes it is dearer
and harder to check. For archives, stay with OCR and use vision for the
exceptions.

## Risks

- **Hallucination** — the model fills in a missing company ID.
- **Prompt injection** — the document can contain an instruction that
  changes the extraction.
- **An OCR error** — a digit read wrongly.
- **Silent failure** — the model returns JSON, but with a wrong value.
- **Automation without a check** — the error reaches the books.

## When not to use it

- **Without validation and a check** — never on invoices and contracts.
- **On poor scans** — OCR first, extraction second.
- **On documents missing key fields** — the model will not invent them
  correctly.
- **On legal interpretation** — extracting fields yes, interpreting no.

## What you need

- **Model:** Qwen3.8 Flash Next with tool calling or structured output.
- **Engine:** vLLM with guided decoding, or llama.cpp with a grammar.
- **Validation:** a JSON schema and a checking script.
- **A person:** for approval and corrections.
