Translation and proofreading are tasks a local model can handle well,
provided you have a **glossary**, a **style** and a **check**. For short
texts one prompt is often enough; for longer documents you have to work
in parts and watch consistency.

## Short answer

- **Translation is not word for word.** Style, terms and context
  decide.
- **A glossary is the foundation** — without it the model mixes
  synonyms.
- **Proofreading should be conservative** — do not change facts and
  names.
- **For legal and medical texts** a person is required.

## Translation procedure

1. **Set the direction and the purpose** — Czech to English, English to
   Czech, marketing, law, technical.
2. **Build a glossary** — key terms and their translations.
3. **Split the text** into paragraphs or sections.
4. **Translate in parts** with the same glossary and style.
5. **Unify the terminology** across the document.
6. **Read it as a whole** — fluency and logic.

## Style and form

| Decision | What to choose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Formality** | “vy” for authorities and clients | „Vaše objednávka“ (your order) |
| **Ty/vy** | one form throughout | do not mix |
| **Terminology** | per the glossary | „smluvní strana“ |
| **Abbreviations** | explain at first use | „DPH (daň z přidané hodnoty)“ |
| **Numbers** | Czech formatting | `10 000,50` |
| **Dates** | one format | `10. 11. 2026` |

Czech distinguishes an informal “ty” and a formal “vy”, roughly like
“tu” and “vous” in French. English has only “you”, so an English
original does not tell you which one to use: the relationship and the
document type do. Pick one and keep it.

## Proofreading

Proofreading is not rewriting. The model should fix:

- grammar and diacritics,
- punctuation,
- word order,
- repetition,
- stylistic clumsiness.

It must not change:

- facts, numbers and names,
- legal meaning,
- quotations,
- the structure of the document.

When in doubt, ask for **a list of changes** and go through them.

## Glossary

A glossary is a table:

| Source (Czech) | Target (English) | Note |
|---|---|---|
| smluvní strana | contracting party | law |
| zhotovitel | contractor | law |
| objednatel | client | law |
| DPH | VAT | accounting |
| výpovědní lhůta | notice period | law |

Without a glossary the model uses synonyms and the document loses
consistency.

## The glossary in practice

Do not hand the glossary to the model as a long block of text. Put it in
the system prompt or as a table in every step. For longer documents, use
**the same glossary** for all parts; otherwise the terms drift apart.

When the model does not know a term, let it **flag** it rather than
invent a translation. An unknown term is better than a wrong one.

## Consistency check

- **The same term** throughout the document.
- **The same form** — “vy” vs. “Vy”.
- **The same names** for companies and products.
- **The same formatting** of numbers and dates.
- **The same tone** across sections.

Watch consistency in machine translation too; the model does not hold it
by itself.

## How to evaluate

- **Fidelity** — nothing important is missing and nothing is added.
- **Terminology** — per the glossary.
- **Fluency** — the text sounds natural.
- **Style** — it suits the purpose.
- **Numbers and names** — unchanged.
- **Consistency** — the same term throughout.

For longer texts, **back-translation** and comparison help, but they are
not proof. The best check is a person who knows both languages.

## How to measure translation quality

An impression is not enough, because a bad translation sounds fluent.
Take 30–50 sentences and count **sentences without an error**:

```
score = sentences without an error / all sentences
```

Sort the errors into categories, because they are fixed differently:

| Category | Example | Who fixes it |
|---|---|---|
| **Meaning** | “výpověď” (notice) swapped for “odstoupení” (withdrawal) | the translator |
| **Terminology** | a different term from the glossary | the glossary |
| **Numbers and dates** | 10 000 → 1 000 | a check against the source |
| **Grammar** | case or agreement | proofreading |
| **Style** | ty/vy, formality | the prompt |

When the errors are mostly terminology, the glossary helps. When they
are in meaning, the model is not good enough for that field. This is a
**calculation** from manually marked sentences, not an automatic
score.

## Batch translation

For longer documents, allow for the length of the answer: a 10-page
document is roughly 25–30 thousand characters, which is 7–9 thousand
tokens. At 50 tok/s that is **2.5–3 minutes** of pure generation per
version.

Checking and a second version after proofreading come on top.
Translating two such documents a day is realistic; a whole book at once
is not.

## When a person is better

- **Legal contracts** — meaning and liability.
- **Medical texts** — the risk is high.
- **Marketing slogans** — creativity and context.
- **Culturally loaded texts** — allusions, idioms, humour.
- **Publications** — reputation.

The model is a good first draft and a helper, not the final translator.

## Local or cloud

Local translation makes sense for **sensitive documents** you do not
want to send out. The cloud can be better for **rare languages** and
**creative** texts. A combination often pays off: a local draft, a
human final edit.
