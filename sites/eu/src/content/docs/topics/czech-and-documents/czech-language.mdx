**Czech** is not just “English with hooks on the letters”. It has rich
morphology, free word order and a strong formal register. A model that
answers fluently in English can look fine in Czech while mixing up
names, numbers and cases. Quality therefore cannot be judged from one
impression.

## Short answer

- **Fluency is not accuracy.** The model can write a pretty Czech
  sentence with the wrong number or name.
- **Czech can cost more tokens** than English; a longer text means more
  tokens and more context.
- **Test tasks, not a language in general.** Summaries, extraction and
  translation have different criteria.

## Tokenization and diacritics

The model does not see letters, it sees tokens — the chunks of text a
tokenizer splits words into. In Czech, words with diacritics can split
into more pieces than ordinary English words. Two consequences:

- **Longer input** — the same text can take more tokens.
- **Bigger context** — a longer token array means a larger KV cache
  (the memory that holds the conversation) and slower prefill (reading
  the prompt before answering).

The exact ratio depends on the tokenizer. When it matters, measure it in
the engine, not by guesswork.

## What to evaluate

| Property | What to watch | Example of a failure |
|---|---|---|
| **Fluency** | natural word order, no calques | “provést rozhodnutí” instead of “rozhodnout” (a literal “make a decision”) |
| **Grammar** | case, gender, agreement | wrong case on a surname |
| **Diacritics** | háčky and čárky in names | “Řízení” written as “Rizeni” |
| **Terminology** | legal, medical, technical terms | “žaloba” (lawsuit) vs. “stížnost” (complaint) |
| **Facts** | numbers, dates, names, quotations | wrong amount or company ID |
| **Style** | formal vs. informal | “ty” in an official letter |
| **Consistency** | the same term throughout | “smlouva” once, “kontrakt” later |

## What we know about it

Qwen3.8 Flash Next is a multilingual, multimodal model. The model card
lists benchmarks for English and code, but **there is no public Czech
benchmark we could point to**. That does not mean the model is bad — it
means you have to verify quality on your own data.

For Czech quality, the following holds:

- **More data means better results**, but Czech is a minority language.
- **Quantization can blur small distinctions** — numbers, names,
  endings.
- **Long context can weaken attention** — the model may skip a passage
  in the middle.
- **Vision helps with scans**, but OCR quality decides the outcome.

## How to test

1. **Pick 20–50 tasks** from real work: summaries, extraction, e-mail,
   proofreading, translation, a question over a document.
2. **Write the correct answers** or the criteria. Not just “like/dislike”.
3. **Use the same quantization and context** you will operate.
4. **Set temperature to 0** for repeatability; creative tasks separately.
5. **Watch four things:** facts, numbers, quotations, grammar.
6. **Keep the result** and repeat after a change of model or
   quantization.

The detailed procedure is in
[quality evaluation](/topics/czech-and-documents/quality-evaluation/).

## A ten-minute Czech test

Before building a large set, run ten phenomena. It is not a benchmark,
it is a filter for whether it is worth continuing.

| Phenomenon | What to enter | What to watch |
|---|---|---|
| Declining names | „Napiš e-mail panu Novákovi o schůzce s Petrou Dvořákovou.“ (Write an e-mail to Mr Novák about a meeting with Petra Dvořáková.) | surname endings |
| Diacritics | „Přepiš větu: Ředitel nařídil řízení.“ (Rewrite: The director ordered the proceedings.) | háčky and čárky |
| Gender and agreement | „Popiš, co udělala firma a co její jednatel.“ (Describe what the company did and what its director did.) | verb agreement |
| Numerals | „Vypiš částky 1 250,50 a 3 000 slovy.“ (Write the amounts out in words.) | correct forms |
| Formality | „Napiš odmítnutí klientovi.“ (Write a refusal to a client.) | consistent formal “vy” |
| Terminology | „Vysvětli rozdíl mezi výpovědí a odstoupením.“ (Explain the difference between notice and withdrawal.) | precision of terms |
| Word order | „Přelož větu s důrazem na termín.“ (Translate a sentence with emphasis on the deadline.) | naturalness |
| Abbreviations | „Co znamená DUZP a kdy se uvádí?“ (What does DUZP mean and when is it stated?) | correct expansion |
| Long text | 3 pages and a question about a detail in the middle | attention |
| Stability | the same question twice | the same answer |

DUZP is the Czech date of taxable supply — the day a VAT event occurs.
Every country has its own equivalent, and the model should know the
local one or say that it does not.

When the model fails in three or more points, do not tune the prompt —
choose another quantization or another model. When it fails in one, that
is a specific task for a person to check.

## What to record from the test

- **Quantization and engine** — without them the result does not hold.
- **Temperature** — 0 for repeatability.
- **Context length** — for the long test.
- **The exact sentences** — so you can repeat the test.
- **Who graded it** — one person or two.

## Three levels of testing

| Level | What you learn | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| **Quick test** | 5 tasks, a basic impression | minutes |
| **Pilot test** | 20–50 tasks, a rubric | hours |
| **Production test** | real documents, weeks | ongoing |

A pilot test is enough for a purchase decision. For deployment you need
production checks, because both documents and models change.

## How to read the results

- **An average is not enough** — look at the worst tasks.
- **Factual errors** are worse than stylistic ones.
- **Stability** matters; the model should not say yes once and no the
  next time.
- **Change context** one step at a time, not everything at once.
- **Compare with a baseline** — a person, the cloud, an older model.

## Where Czech fails most often

- **Declining names and titles** — the model may use the wrong case.
- **Numbers and codes** — company IDs, VAT IDs, amounts, dates.
- **Diacritics in proper names** — it may “correct” a surname.
- **The formal register** — it mixes “ty” and “vy”.
- **Terminology** — it uses synonyms that are not synonyms in law.
- **Long documents** — it skips or garbles a passage.

## When quality is good enough

- **Internal summaries** — when a person reads and checks them.
- **An e-mail draft** — when you edit it before sending.
- **Translation for orientation** — when it is not meant for
  publication.
- **Extraction with validation** — when numbers are checked against the
  source.

For legal documents, medical records and financial reporting a person
is always required.
