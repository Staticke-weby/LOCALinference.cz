Summarising is the most common task people want to solve locally. It is
also the task where a model most often “looks good” while dropping an
important sentence. A Czech document adds declension, terminology and a
formal style on top of that.

## Short answer

- **A short document** fits in the context; a long one is better
  summarised in parts.
- **Always state the purpose of the summary** — for whom, how long,
  what it must contain.
- **Check facts, numbers and names** against the source.
- **128 GB is enough** for documents and models; the limit is context
  and quality.

## Procedure

1. **Choose the document and the purpose.** A lawyer wants a different
   summary from a manager and a customer.
2. **Check the input quality.** A scan without OCR is worse than text.
3. **Split the text** into logical parts — chapters, articles,
   paragraphs.
4. **Summarise each part** with a clear brief and a length.
5. **Join the partial summaries** and let the model remove repetition.
6. **Verify the facts** and add citations to the source.

With a short document you can skip the split and summarise it in one
go. With a long one, splitting is safer.

## Which context to choose

| Document | Context | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| One page | 8k | one summary |
| A few pages | 32k | one or two summaries |
| Tens of pages | 128k | chapter by chapter |
| Hundreds of pages | RAG | chunking and selected passages |

The 262k maximum is not a recommended operating point. The longer the
input, the slower the [prefill](/dictionary/performance/prefill/) and
the less room left for other work.

## A prompt that works

The basic structure:

> You are an assistant for Czech documents. Summarise the text for
> [reader]. Keep the output to [length] and include the main decisions,
> the parties, the deadlines, the amounts and the risks. Do not state
> anything that is not in the text. For numbers and names, say where
> they come from.

Add:

- **the form** — bullets, a table, an e-mail,
- **what it must not do** — invent, change numbers, use synonyms,
- **what it must do** — cite the page or paragraph,
- **the reader** — lawyer, management, client.

Write the prompt in the language of the document, or in English with
the output language stated. The structure stays the same; what changes
is the vocabulary the model reaches for.

## A long document: split it

For a document above 32k tokens, do not produce one summary. Split it
into chapters or articles and summarise each separately. Then:

1. **Merge the partial summaries** into one whole.
2. **Remove repetition** and unify the style.
3. **Add cross-references** — “see Article 5”.
4. **Check what fell out** — especially numbers and pages.

The result is longer than one “clever” summary, but more faithful.

## A sample prompt for a contract

> You are an assistant for Czech contracts. Summarise this contract for
> a manager who does not know legal terminology. Output: 8–12 bullets.
> State the parties, the subject, the price, the duration, the notice
> period and the penalties. Do not invent anything; when a figure is
> missing, write “not in the text”. For numbers, cite the article or
> paragraph.

That gives you a structure you can check.

## How many pages fit in the context

Context is counted in tokens, not pages. An indicative **calculation**
for Czech: at 3.5–4 characters per token, a 32k context holds roughly
112–128 thousand characters, which is **60–70 pages** of ordinary text.
A 128k context holds four times that, 262k eight times.

These are **calculations** with the stated ratio, not measurements of a
particular tokenizer. Verify the real ratio in the engine. At the same
time, **fitting is not handling**: with a longer input the risk of
context rot grows — attention degrading across a long prompt — and the
model may skip a passage in the middle.

## Output template

A summary is usable only when it has a fixed structure. A proven
template for a contract:

```markdown
## Parties
- client: …
- contractor: …

## Subject and price
- subject: …
- price: … (Article X)

## Term and termination
- effective from: …
- notice period: …

## Risks and penalties
- …

## What is not in the document
- …
```

The “what is not in the document” section is more important than it
looks. It forces the model to admit gaps instead of filling them, and
it gives you a place to spot the error.

## Quality check

- [ ] **The main message** is right.
- [ ] **Parties and names** match.
- [ ] **Numbers and dates** match.
- [ ] **Nothing important is missing.**
- [ ] **Nothing extra is invented.**
- [ ] **The style** suits the reader.
- [ ] **Citations** point to the source.

When in doubt, ask the model for **a list of claims with citations** and
go through them against the document.

## Czech specifics

- **Formal vs. informal style** — “vy” in an official document.
- **Terminology** — “smluvní strana” (contracting party), “zhotovitel”
  (contractor), “objednatel” (client).
- **Declension** — names and titles must stay correct.
- **Numbers** — amounts, company IDs, VAT IDs, dates; never round.
- **Abbreviations** — explain them at first use.

## What you need

- **Model:** Qwen3.8 Flash Next on a 128GB box.
- **Engine:** vLLM on DGX/RTX, llama.cpp on Strix/Mac.
- **Context:** 32k for ordinary documents, 128k for longer ones.
- **A check:** a person who knows the document.

## Where the limit is

- **A long document** — the model can skip a passage.
- **A scan without OCR** — input quality decides.
- **Legal and medical text** — a person is required.
- **Quantization** — 4-bit can blur small distinctions.
- **Hallucination** — the model can add a “logical” piece of
  information that is not in the text.
