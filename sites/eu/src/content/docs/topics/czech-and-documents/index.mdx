**Czech** is a different problem for local AI than English is. It has
rich morphology, free word order, diacritics and a formal as well as an
informal register. A model that answers well in English may not be
equally good on Czech contracts, invoices or e-mails. This hub
summarises what to test and how to work with Czech documents.

Most of it applies to any morphologically rich language with a smaller
share of training data — Czech is simply the case we can document, and
the one this site is written from.

## Short answer

- **Czech is not automatically good.** Qwen3.8 Flash Next is
  multilingual, but there is no public Czech benchmark we could cite.
- **The task decides, not the language.** Summaries, extraction and
  translation have different criteria and different risks.
- **Test on your own data.** 20–50 tasks from real work say more than
  one benchmark.

## What makes Czech different

Czech is a West Slavic language with roughly ten million speakers. In
training data it is a minority language, which is why the model can be
fluent and still imprecise.

| Property | Why it complicates the work |
|---|---|
| **Morphology** | words take many forms; the model has to handle case and conjugation |
| **Diacritics** | háčky and čárky change meaning and even surnames |
| **Word order** | free; emphasis and meaning shift with order |
| **Formality** | “ty” vs. “vy”, official vs. everyday style |
| **Terminology** | legal, medical and technical terms are not unified |
| **Numbers and codes** | company registration number, VAT ID, dates, amounts, contract numbers |

The model learns this from data. When it has seen little Czech, it can
be fluent but inaccurate. That is why facts, numbers and quotations in
Czech documents always get checked.

## A test set by task

Before you start, decide what “good” means for you. Different tasks have
different criteria and a different cost of error:

| Task | Samples | What must pass | Cost of error |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Summary | 10 | facts, numbers, nothing extra | medium |
| Extraction | 10 | every field against the source | high |
| Translation | 10 | glossary and meaning | medium |
| Correspondence | 5 | tone and facts | low |
| Question over a document | 10 | the right passage and a citation | high |

45 tasks in total. That is the scope one person can handle in a day,
and enough to show whether the model is good enough for your documents.

## The economics of the test

An illustrative **calculation**: 45 tasks, two generations each, three
minutes of grading per task = roughly **4.5 hours of work**. Against
that stands the risk of deploying a model that gets the amount wrong in
five percent of cases.

When one wrong invoice means an hour of fixing and you process 500
invoices a month, five percent is 25 fixes — 25 hours. A half-day test
pays for itself in the first month.

## What you need

- **Qwen3.8 Flash Next** on a 128GB box — a model that handles both
  documents and tool calling.
- **Enough context** — 32k for ordinary documents, 128k for longer ones.
- **RAG** — when documents accumulate and you do not want to send
  everything at once.
- **A check** — a person who verifies facts and numbers.
- **A record of the configuration** — model, quantization, temperature,
  prompt.

## What we know and what we do not

**We know:**

- Qwen3.8 Flash Next is multimodal and multilingual.
- The model has 180B parameters in total and 6B active.
- Vision handles documents and screenshots.
- Tool calling and structured output are supported.

**We do not know:**

- Exactly how its Czech grammar compares with other models.
- How it performs on Czech legal and accounting documents.
- How quality changes with quantization and context length.

None of that can be read off a model card. You have to test it on your
own tasks. The method is in
[quality evaluation](/topics/czech-and-documents/quality-evaluation/).

## How to start

1. **Pick one task** — summary, extraction, translation.
2. **Take 20–50 samples** from real work, not textbook texts.
3. **Write the criteria** and the correct answers.
4. **Run the model** with the same quantization you will operate.
5. **Write down the results** and decide whether to continue.

## When not to use it

- **Legal interpretation** — the model can write a convincing but wrong
  answer.
- **Medical diagnosis** — the risk is too high.
- **A financial audit without a check** — numbers must be verified.
- **Publication without proofreading** — grammar and style need a person.
- **Documents you do not understand** — you cannot check the output.

## Where the limit is

- **A long document** — the model can skip an important passage.
- **Numbers and codes** — company IDs, VAT IDs and amounts need
  validation.
- **Legal and medical text** — an error has consequences; a person must
  check.
- **Quantization** — 4-bit can blur small distinctions.
- **Context** — 262k is a ceiling, not a recommended operating point.
