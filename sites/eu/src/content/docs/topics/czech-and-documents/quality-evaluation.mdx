Without your own test you do not know whether the model handles **your**
Czech tasks. A benchmark from a model card is useful, but it does not
say how the model will cope with your contracts, invoices or e-mails.
Quality evaluation is therefore part of deployment, not an academic
discipline.

## Short answer

- **20–50 tasks from real work** beat thousands of generic ones.
- **A rubric and correct answers** decide; an impression is not enough.
- **The same model, quantization and context** for all measurements.
- **Keep the results** — without a baseline, improvement cannot be
  shown.

## Building the test set

Choose tasks that really happen:

| Type | Count | What to evaluate |
|---|---:|---|
| Summaries | 5–10 | facts, scope, style |
| Extraction | 5–10 | field accuracy, validation |
| Answer from a document | 5–10 | the right passage, citation |
| Translation | 5–10 | terminology, fluency |
| Correspondence | 5–10 | tone, structure, facts |
| Code / commands | 3–5 | whether it runs, safety |

For each task write down:

- **the input** (text or document),
- **the expected output** or the criteria,
- **what is forbidden** (inventing, changing numbers),
- **who the reader is**.

## The rubric

For each task, score 0–2 in these categories:

| Category | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Facts** | wrong | partly | correct |
| **Numbers and dates** | wrong | uncertain | correct |
| **Citations** | missing | partial | exact |
| **Terminology** | wrong | inconsistent | correct |
| **Grammar** | errors | minor | clean |
| **Form** | unusable | needs edits | finished |

Then convert the total into a percentage. What matters is that **0 means
an error**, not “the model tried”.

## The protocol

1. **Freeze the configuration** — model, quantization, engine, context.
2. **Set temperature to 0** for repeatability; creative tasks
   separately.
3. **Run each task twice** and watch stability.
4. **Record the prompt** and any system message.
5. **Keep the raw outputs** as well as the scores.
6. **Repeat after a change** of model, quantization or prompt.

## What to watch in Czech

- **Diacritics** in names and titles.
- **Declension** of names and terms.
- **The formal register** — a consistent “vy”.
- **Terminology** — per the glossary.
- **Numbers and dates** — Czech formatting.
- **Length** — the model can pad the answer.

## A sample rubric for a contract

| Criterion | 0 points | 1 point | 2 points |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parties | missing | one | both correct |
| Subject | missing | general | precise |
| Price | missing/wrong | partly | correct |
| Duration | missing | approximate | correct |
| Notice | missing | mentioned | correct |
| Risks | missing | general | concrete |

Maximum 12 points. For deployment, set a threshold below which you do
not use the output without a manual fix.

## How to record the results

- **Date and model version.**
- **Quantization and engine.**
- **Temperature and context.**
- **Prompt and glossary.**
- **Score per task.**
- **Notes on the errors.**

Keep them in a table or in Markdown. Without a record the test cannot be
repeated and the changes cannot be explained.

## Calculating the score and the threshold

The rubric gives points, but a decision needs a number. An illustrative
**calculation** for 30 tasks with six categories at 0–2 points:

```
maximum = 30 tasks × 6 categories × 2 points = 360
score = points earned / 360
```

Example: 300 points is 83 %. That alone does not say whether to deploy
the model. The **distribution** decides: when all 60 lost points fall on
“numbers and dates”, 83 % is dangerous. When they fall on “style”, it is
usable with proofreading.

The practical threshold follows from the risk:

| Task | Threshold | If not met |
|---|---:|---|
| Internal summaries | 75 % | check every output |
| Field extraction | 95 % on numbers | another model, or by hand |
| Answer from a document | 90 % on citations | RAG, or by hand |
| Translation for publication | 95 % | a person |

## How long it takes

For 30 tasks and two runs: 60 generations, each in the range of seconds
to minutes, plus manual grading. Realistically **half a day to a day**
of work when you have the criteria up front. Without criteria it drags,
because you decide again for every task what correct means.

## Common evaluation mistakes

- **Testing on training data** — the result is inflated.
- **Changing several things at once** — you do not know what helped.
- **Grading a single prompt** — chance.
- **Comparing different quantizations** — different numbers, different
  quality.
- **Trusting one benchmark** — it does not say how your tasks go.
- **Not saving the results** — nothing can be repeated.

## How many tasks are enough

As a guide, **20–50 tasks**. When the results vary a lot between tasks,
add more. When they are stable, a smaller set is enough. The goal is not
a statistical publication, but a decision on whether to deploy the
model.

## When to repeat the test

- **A change of model** or version.
- **A change of quantization** — NVFP4 vs. FP8 vs. GGUF.
- **A change of context** — 32k vs. 128k.
- **A change of prompt** or glossary.
- **A change of documents** — new types of contracts or invoices.
