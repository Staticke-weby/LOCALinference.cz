**Context engineering** is the discipline of assembling what the model
sees in a given request: system prompt, tools, documents, history and
the question. It is not just "writing a better prompt". It is a token
budget and an order, and both decide quality.

## Short answer

- **Context is a limited budget.** Every part costs something.
- **Order matters.** In the middle of a long text, information gets
  lost.
- **Less is more.** A relevant chunk beats a whole document.
- **Compress after measuring**, not pre-emptively.

## What goes into the context

| Part | Typical tokens | What to do |
|---|---:|---|
| system prompt | 500–2,000 | short and specific |
| tool schemas | 1,000–3,000 | fewer tools |
| documents | 2,000–20,000 | only relevant chunks |
| history | 0–20,000 | summarise the old part |
| question | 50–500 | make it clear |

The total has to fit the window and leave room for the answer.

## The system prompt

A good system prompt is short, specific and stable. A template that
works:

```text
You are an assistant for commercial contracts.
Answer in English, briefly, citing the article number.
If the answer is not in the text, write "not found".
Do not guess and do not fill in missing facts.
```

The rules "cite the article" and "not found" matter more than a long
personality description. You save hundreds of tokens and get
verifiable answers.

## Order and attention

The model is not a perfect reader. Information at the beginning and
the end of a text is found more reliably than in the middle. So:

- **key instructions at the top**,
- **citations and sources next to chunks**,
- **the most relevant document first**,
- **the question at the end**,
- **split long text**.

## Example: a contract and a question

Say you are working with one contract and want five answers from it.
The naive approach sends the whole document with every request.
Better:

| Step | What to send | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | the question and the contract outline | the model knows where to look |
| 2 | only the relevant sections | less noise |
| 3 | the quote and the article number | verifiability |
| 4 | a short summary of previous answers | continuity |

The model gets thousands of tokens instead of hundreds of thousands.
The answer can be checked against the article number, and you do not
pay for the whole document five times.

## A context budget

Qwen3.8 Flash Next has a native maximum of **262,144 tokens** and a
recommended operating point of **131,072 tokens** (128K). The maximum
is a ceiling, not a target: quality and speed are better at shorter
context.

An illustrative **calculation** for a 128K window:

| Item | Tokens |
|---|---:|
| system prompt | 1,500 |
| tool schemas | 2,000 |
| documents | 80,000 |
| history | 10,000 |
| question | 300 |
| reserve for the answer | 8,000 |
| **total** | **~102,000** |

That fits into 131,072 with room to spare. When the documents grow to
150,000 tokens, they no longer fit and must be selected or compressed.

Words per token depends on the tokenizer. For English, one token is
roughly three quarters of a word; check the exact number with the
model's tokenizer rather than guessing.

## Compression

- **Summarise history** every N steps.
- **Trim tool output** — the first few kB are usually enough.
- **Deduplicate** identical chunks.
- **Move to memory** what you do not need right now.

Compression has a price: you lose detail. Do it when the context
really overflows, and measure what was lost.

## How to measure it

1. **Baseline** — 20 tasks with the current context.
2. **Change one thing** — order, number of chunks, prompt length.
3. **Quality** — correctness, citations, refusals.
4. **Cost and latency** — tokens and TTFT.
5. **Write it down** — what changed and why.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why it happens | How to avoid it |
|---|---|---|
| Sending the whole document every time | convenience | select chunks |
| A 5,000-token system prompt | "cover everything" | 500–2,000 is enough |
| History without a limit | the agent remembers everything | summarise every N steps |
| The question at the start | human habit | question at the end |
| Long text without structure | copied from a PDF | headings and citations |
| Compressing without measuring | "we will save tokens" | baseline first |

## When not to bother

- A short chat under 8k.
- One task and one document.
- When the model already fails on quality.
- When there is no test set.
