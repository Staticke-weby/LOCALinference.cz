> **This is not a recommendation.** We list examples of publishers and
> criteria, not a ranking. A link to a repository does not mean the
> model is safe or suitable.

Abliterated models do not come out of big-company labs. They are
published by individuals, small teams and community publishers, often
within days of the base model's release. Quality therefore varies a
lot.

## Short answer

- **The publisher is not the same thing as the model vendor.** Most just
  modify finished weights.
- **Provenance is everything**: base, revision, method, quantisation.
- **The indexes are a catalogue**, not proof. They do not verify claims.
- **"Uncensored" in the name is not a technical fact.**

## Who does it

| Type of publisher | What they do | What to watch |
|---|---|---|
| **An individual** | quick builds, experiments | documentation, licence |
| **A small team** | consistent methodology, measurements | revisions, tests |
| **A quantisation publisher** | converts someone else's model to NVFP4/GGUF | preserving the abliteration |
| **A community index** | catalogue and metadata | verifies nothing, only collects |

In practice the roles mix: one publisher abliterates the model, another
quantises it, a third packages it into a container. Something can get
lost at every step.

## What a model card should contain

A solid card answers seven questions:

1. **The base** — the exact revision of Qwen3.8 Flash Next.
2. **The method** — diff-in-means, orthogonalisation, LoRA, Heretic.
3. **Where the edit was made** — attention, experts, MTP.
4. **Refusal before and after** — set, scorer, protocol.
5. **Quality** — benchmarks and known losses.
6. **Quantisation** — what it was measured on.
7. **Licence** — the publisher and the base model.

When one is missing, it is a candidate. When three are missing, it is a
risk.

## Provenance in practice

Provenance means you can trace the path from the base to the file:

```
Qwen/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (revision A)
  └─ nvidia/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-FP4-Local (revision B)
       └─ DevelopingDad/...-Abliterated (revision C)
```

At every step it should be visible what changed and what stayed. When the
card says only "based on Qwen", provenance is missing.

## Indexes and catalogues

- [abliteratedmodels.org](https://www.abliteratedmodels.org/) —
  metadata from the Hugging Face API, methodology, filtering.
- [abliter.cn](https://abliter.cn/en/) — a similar catalogue with formats
  and methods.

Both collect public metadata. They **do not verify** whether the model
really does what the card claims. Treat them as a signpost, not an
audit.

## How a build is made

The typical path from the base to a finished repository:

1. **Base** — the exact Qwen3.8 revision is downloaded.
2. **Abliteration** — computing the direction and editing the weights.
3. **Quantisation** — NVFP4, GGUF or MLX, often with a test of whether
   the edit survived.
4. **Package** — container, launcher, PLE and MTP configuration.
5. **Documentation** — method, numbers, licence, date.

Every step can change the result. When the card skips step 3, you do not
know whether the model still refuses less after quantisation.

## How to check a repository

- [ ] Is the exact revision of the base stated?
- [ ] Are the method and the scope of the edit described?
- [ ] Are the refusal numbers before and after?
- [ ] Is it stated which quantisation was measured?
- [ ] Are both the publisher's and the base licence visible?
- [ ] Is there a configuration in the repo for the engine you use?
- [ ] Is there a date and a version?

When more than two items are missing, treat the model as an experiment.

## Common publisher mistakes

| Mistake | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Abliteration on BF16, selling NVFP4 without a test | refusal comes back |
| No launcher for the specific hardware | the model cannot be started |
| Quantisation not stated with the numbers | the numbers cannot be compared |
| A promise of "same as the base" without measurement | untrustworthy |
| A licence copied from the base without explanation | legal risk |

## Who is who

| Role | What they do | Responsible for |
|---|---|---|
| **The base vendor** | trains Qwen3.8 | architecture and licence |
| **The quantiser** | converts to NVFP4/GGUF | format and numbers |
| **The abliterator** | removes refusal | method and measurement |
| **The packager** | launcher, container, PLE | that it can be started |
| **The index** | catalogue and metadata | verifies none of it |

One entity can fill several roles. When the card says "by X", you need to
find out which role X actually had.

## How to recognise a repackaged build

- **The original model card has disappeared** and only the name is left.
- **The base revision or the method is missing.**
- **The numbers are repeated** from another repo without change.
- **The launcher is from different hardware** and nobody says so.
- **The licence is "other"** without a link to the text.
- **The date is old**, but the name promises something new.

A repackaged build is not automatically fraud. But it is something
different from original work, and the card should admit that.

## Warning signs

- **"Uncensored" without a method** — marketing.
- **A missing licence** or one that conflicts with the base.
- **No refusal numbers** before and after.
- **Claims parity without a configuration.**
- **A gated repo without explaining who published it and why.**
- **Binaries instead of weights** — you do not know what they contain.
- **An aggressive name** instead of a technical description.

## Example: four roles

| Example | Role | What is documented |
|---|---|---|
| huihui-ai | abliteration of the base | BF16 weights, GGUF variants |
| OrcaRouter | abliteration + NVFP4 | 180B, 184 GB, a card with measurements |
| lflare | editing on the quantisation grid | CSF container, quality measurements |
| DevelopingDad | a package for DGX Spark | launcher, PLE from SSD, measurements |

These are not recommendations. They are examples of different roles that
you must be able to tell apart when you read a card.

## When not to buy it

- When the card does not state a licence.
- When there are no refusal numbers.
- When you do not know which quantisation was measured.
- When the publisher mixes marketing and engineering.
- When the model needs more memory than you have.
