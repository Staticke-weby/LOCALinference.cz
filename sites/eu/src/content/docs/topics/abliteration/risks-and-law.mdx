> **This is not legal advice.** It is an overview of the risks and the
> questions that have to be settled before deployment. Consult a lawyer
> about a specific case.

Abliteration is not just a technical edit. It changes which outputs the
model produces, and with that the risk for you, your users and the
people the model makes decisions about. Liability stays with the
operator.

## Short answer

- **A model has no will and no intent.** The risk arises with the user
  and the operator.
- **The biggest risk is the combination with tools.** An agent can
  perform an action, not just write text.
- **Local operation means neither anonymity nor GDPR compliance.**
- **The licence differs from build to build** and some cards contradict
  each other.

## What a model can cause

| Risk | What it looks like | Who carries the consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Illegal content** | instructions, manufacture, distribution | the operator and the user |
| **Harm to health** | dangerous advice, a procedure | the operator |
| **Disinformation** | convincing invented text | whoever publishes it |
| **Hate** | dehumanisation of groups | the operator |
| **Privacy** | assembling personal data | the data controller |
| **Harm by an agent** | an email, a payment, a deletion | the operator |
| **Discredit** | text passed off as expert | whoever uses it |

A model on its own executes nothing. But when it gets tools, it can send
an email, write to a database or run a command. That is where the risk
stops being textual.

## Capability versus intent

An abliterated model is **more capable of doing harm**, because it has no
guardrails. But it is not an "evil model". The difference is that:

- a model has no intent, only probabilities,
- a user has intent and responsibility,
- an operator has control and responsibility,
- the publisher of the weights is responsible for what it claims.

When these roles get mixed up, the impression arises that "the model did
it".

## Liability

- **The operator** decides the purpose, access and deployment.
- **The user** is responsible for what they do with the output.
- **The publisher of the weights** is responsible for the model
  description and licence, not for your use.
- **The hardware vendor** is not responsible for the model's outputs.

With an agent there is also an **action approver**. When an agent sends an
email, the one who authorised the sending is responsible, not the model.

## AI Act

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 introduces obligations according to the type
and risk of the system. For a local model that means:

- **classify the purpose** — what the system decides and about whom,
- **document** the model, the data and the limitations,
- **human oversight** for decisions affecting people's rights,
- **transparency** towards users,
- **risk management** and testing,
- **incidents** and reporting according to the type of system.

Not every internal chatbot is high-risk. Deployment in recruitment,
education, healthcare or access to services can be. Check the current
text and annexes; the interpretation can change. If you operate outside
the EU, your local rules apply instead, but the same questions remain.

## GDPR

Abliteration does not change the obligations. The following still
applies:

- **a legal basis** for processing,
- **minimisation** — do not send the model what you do not need,
- **retention** — how long you keep logs and outputs,
- **data subject rights** — access, rectification, erasure,
- **security** — access, encryption, backups.

Watch out for **logs**: they can contain whole prompts and personal
data. Switch them off and you lose the audit trail; switch them on and
you raise the risk. The solution is short retention and logging
metadata, not content.

## Risk matrix by use

| Use | Suitability | What must be there |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal research and testing** | suitable | documentation, isolation, no production data |
| **Creative writing for adults** | conditional | age verification, moderation, a legal framework |
| **Customer support** | conditional | output moderation, human oversight, logs |
| **HR and recruitment** | **unsuitable** | it decides about people, high-risk |
| **Healthcare and law** | **unsuitable** | professional liability |
| **A public service** | **unsuitable** | transparency and user protection |

"Conditional" means the model must not be the only layer. There must be
moderation, a human and the option to stop operation.

## What to do in case of misuse

- **Detection** — patterns of repeated queries, complaints, logs.
- **Stopping** — immediate shutdown of the model or a specific account.
- **Evidence** — keep logs, but without unnecessary personal data.
- **Notification** — according to the nature and to GDPR/AI Act.
- **Remediation** — moderation, restrictions, training.
- **Record** — what happened and what changed.

When misuse means illegal content, deleting it is not enough. The
reporting obligation has to be assessed.

## When the base model is the better choice

- When you need **reliable tools** and format.
- When it is a **public service**.
- When you have **no moderation**.
- When the risk is **higher than the benefit**.
- When you are **not sure about the licence**.

## Checklist before deployment

- [ ] Is the purpose clear and who is responsible for it?
- [ ] Is the model suitable for that purpose, even without guardrails?
- [ ] Is the licence settled for commercial use?
- [ ] Do we have input and output moderation?
- [ ] Is a human in control of sensitive actions?
- [ ] Are the logs free of unnecessary personal data?
- [ ] Can we stop the model and revoke access?
- [ ] Do we have an incident plan?
- [ ] Is it clear what "this is not legal advice" covers?

## Incident

1. **Stop** the model or the agent.
2. **Revoke** tokens and access.
3. **Preserve** the logs and establish the scope.
4. **Fix** the cause and add a test.
5. **Consider notification** under GDPR and the AI Act.
6. **Write down** the lessons.

## When not to use it

- **A public service** without moderation.
- **Healthcare, law, HR** without professional oversight.
- **Decisions about people** without human control.
- **An agent with production keys.**
- **When you are not sure about the licence.**
