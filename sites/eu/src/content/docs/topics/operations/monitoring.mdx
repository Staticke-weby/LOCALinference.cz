Monitoring is not just a graph. It is how you find out that the model is
**slowing down**, **running out of memory** or that **someone sent an
oversized request** — before the users notice.

## Short answer

- **Watch tok/s, TTFT, the queue and memory.** Those are the four basic
  numbers.
- **Add temperature and throttling** on laptops and small boxes.
- **Log errors** and restarts.
- **Set alerts from a baseline**, not from a feeling.

## What to measure

| Metric | Why | When it is a problem |
|---|---|---|
| **Decode tok/s** | speed of the answer | a sustained drop |
| **TTFT** | waiting for the first token | growth with context or queue |
| **Prefill tok/s** | reading the document | a drop after an update |
| **Queue** | how many people are waiting | it keeps growing |
| **KV cache** | memory for context | approaching the limit |
| **GPU / RAM** | total memory | OOM or swap |
| **Temperature** | throttling | high under load |
| **Errors** | stability | 5xx, timeouts |

## How to collect it

- **vLLM** has metrics and a Prometheus endpoint; use the official
  documentation.
- **The llama.cpp server** logs requests and speeds.
- **NVIDIA:** `nvidia-smi` or DCGM.
- **AMD:** `rocm-smi`.
- **System:** CPU, RAM, disk, network.

Store metrics with a timestamp and the model, otherwise they cannot be
compared.

## Baseline

First measure the **normal state**:

- one user, a short prompt,
- one user, a 32k document,
- four users, short prompts,
- a long run (hours).

Only then set alerts. Without a baseline you do not know what "slow"
means.

## Alerts

- **TTFT** above your limit for several minutes.
- The **queue** grows and does not drain.
- **Memory** below a safe reserve.
- **Temperature** leading to throttling.
- **Error rate** above the usual level.
- A **restart** of the model or the server.

Send alerts to email, chat or a ticket. A dashboard nobody opens is not
enough.

## Logs

- **Who** and **when**.
- The **model** and the **version**.
- **Prompt and answer length**.
- **Latency**.
- The **error**, without secrets.

Logs need retention and access rights. They can contain personal data.

## Example: a queue alert

Normally there are 0–2 requests in the queue. The alert fires when the
queue is longer than 10 for five minutes. That means the model cannot
keep up or someone sent an overlong context. Without a baseline you
would not know whether 10 is "a lot".

## Thresholds and alerts in practice

An alert should be tied to impact, not to a number. A practical table:

| Signal | Warning | Critical | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| TTFT | 2× baseline | 5× baseline | check the queue and context |
| Queue | > 5 requests for 5 min | > 15 requests | lower concurrency |
| Decode | −20 % against baseline | −50 % | temperature, throttling, version |
| Free memory | < 15 % | < 5 % | lower context or batch |
| Error rate | > 2 % | > 10 % | restart, rollback |
| Temperature | 85 °C | 95 °C | cooling, power limit |

The numbers are **indicative**; measure your own baseline. An alert
that fires every day and means nothing stops interesting people — and
then they miss the real one.

## How much space the logs take

Logging is cheap until you start storing the whole prompt. An
illustrative **calculation**: a record without content is around 500
bytes. At 5,000 requests a day that is **2.5 MB a day**, roughly 75 MB
a month. Add whole prompts and answers and it can be a hundred times
more.

So keep two levels: **metrics and metadata** for a long time, **content**
briefly or not at all. Keys and full personal data do not belong in the
logs.

## What not to do

- **Alerts without a baseline** — you will tune a threshold instead of
  running the service.
- **A dashboard without an owner** — nobody looks at it.
- **Logging everything** — cost growth and leak risk.
- **Measuring only the average** — p95 reveals problems the average
  hides.
- **Forgetting throttling** — performance drops while the error rate
  stays at zero.

## Checklist

- [ ] Are the metrics available?
- [ ] Is there a baseline?
- [ ] Are there alerts?
- [ ] Are the logs free of secrets?
- [ ] Is there retention?
- [ ] Is there a dashboard?
- [ ] Is there an incident response plan?

## When you do not need this

- One user and a short test.
- The model serves nobody else.
- You have no production data.
- The terminal log is enough for you.
