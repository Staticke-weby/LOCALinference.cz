Running locally means you do not send data to the cloud. **It does not
mean GDPR or the AI Act stop applying to you.** You still process
personal data on your own box, and the model can be part of a system
that the law regulates.

> This is a practical overview, not legal advice. Discuss a specific
> deployment with a lawyer or a DPO. Czech and EU rules apply.

## Short answer

- **Local ≠ unregulated.** GDPR applies to data on your disk too.
- **Legal basis** and **minimisation** are the first questions.
- **The AI Act** depends on the type of deployment and the risk; not
  every model is "high-risk".
- **Documentation, logs and human oversight** are practical obligations.

## GDPR

| Question | What you need |
|---|---|
| **Legal basis** | consent, contract, legitimate interest… |
| **Minimisation** | only the data you need |
| **Retention** | how long, and why |
| **Access** | who sees what |
| **Data subject rights** | access, rectification, erasure |
| **Processor** | who processes the data |
| **DPIA** | for high-risk processing |
| **Security** | technical and organisational measures |

A local box is a processing tool. You still need a purpose, a basis and
rules.

## The AI Act

The AI Act introduces obligations according to the **type and risk** of
the system. For local AI that means:

- **classify** the use case and the risk,
- **document** the model, the data and the purpose,
- **human oversight** for sensitive decisions,
- **transparency** towards users,
- **risk management** and testing,
- **incidents** and reporting according to the type of system.

Not every internal chatbot is high-risk. But a medical, HR or scoring
deployment can be. Check the current text and its annexes.

## Local specifics

- **The data stays**, but it can sit in logs, the index and backups.
- **The model** may come from a third party; its license and terms
  apply.
- **An agent** can send data out through a tool even though the model
  runs locally.
- **Prompt injection** can get around your rules.
- **Deletion** must cover documents, vectors and logs.

## Documentation

- **Purpose** and legal basis.
- **Categories** of data and of data subjects.
- **Model** and version.
- **Architecture** and data flows.
- **Permissions** and roles.
- **Retention** and deletion.
- **Tests** and incidents.
- **Human oversight**.

## Example: a medical practice

A practice wants a local model to transcribe notes. Even though the data
stays in the practice, it is health data: it needs a legal basis,
minimisation, retention, permissions and a risk assessment. The local
box is the technical part, not the whole solution.

## Who is who

With local AI the roles get confused. What decides is who determines the
purpose and who has access to the data:

| Role | Who that is | What follows |
|---|---|---|
| **Controller** | your company | purpose, basis, data subject rights |
| **Processor** | whoever processes data for you | a contract and instructions |
| **Weights supplier** | the model maker | often **not** a processor |
| **Infrastructure supplier** | a cloud, if you use one | a processor |
| **DPO** | data protection officer | oversight and advice |

A model downloaded from Hugging Face and run on your box does not mean
the maker sees your data. A processor is whoever has access to the data
— typically a cloud operator or an external administrator.

## AI Act: indicative milestones

The obligations are introduced in stages. Under Regulation (EU)
2024/1689 the following milestones are usually cited:

| Date | What starts to apply |
|---|---|
| 1 August 2024 | the regulation entered into force |
| 2 February 2025 | the prohibitions of certain practices |
| 2 August 2025 | obligations for general-purpose models (GPAI) |
| 2 August 2026 | selected high-risk systems (Annex III) |
| 2 August 2027 | high-risk in regulated products (Annex I) |

The dates can change through amendments. Before deciding, check the
current consolidated text and its annexes; this overview is not legal
advice.

## An erasure request in practice

With RAG and logs, deleting the document is not enough. The procedure:

1. **The document** — the original in storage.
2. **The vectors** — the chunks in the index.
3. **The cache** — the prefix cache and temporary files.
4. **The logs** — if they contain the text or identifiers.
5. **The backups** — according to the retention plan; confirm when it
   disappears.
6. **Confirmation** — record who performed the erasure and when.

Without steps 2 and 3 the text stays "alive" in answers even though the
file is gone. That is a common finding in an audit.

## Checklist

- [ ] Do we have a legal basis?
- [ ] Do we minimise the data?
- [ ] Do we know everywhere the data lives?
- [ ] Can the data be deleted?
- [ ] Do we have documentation?
- [ ] Do we have human oversight?
- [ ] Do we have a risk assessment?
- [ ] Do we have an incident plan?

## When to call a lawyer

- You process **personal data** on a larger scale.
- You deploy in **healthcare, HR or finance**.
- An agent **decides** or recommends with an impact on people.
- The data is a **special category**.
- You do not know whether you are high-risk.
