The embedding model decides whether RAG **finds the right chunk**. It is
not a chat model and it does not need 128 GB. It is a small model that
turns text into a vector. A bad choice shows up as "RAG ignores my
questions", even when the pipeline is technically fine.

## Short answer

- **Choose by language and task**, not by size.
- **A non-English corpus needs a multilingual or language-specific
  model**; an English-only model can work, but quality differs.
- **Test on 20 questions** and watch whether the right chunk is in the
  top 3–5.
- **When you change the model or the chunking, regenerate the index.**

## What the model does

The model turns text into a vector. Similar texts end up with nearby
vectors. Similarity is usually measured as cosine similarity. The model
has:

- **a maximum input length** — longer text is truncated or split,
- **a vector dimension** — it affects storage and speed,
- **a language** — some models are English-only, others multilingual,
- **a licence** — commercial use.

The right model depends on your data. For non-English documents, look
for a multilingual model and test it on your own questions.

## How to choose

| Criterion | What to check |
|---|---|
| **Language** | your language, English, a mix |
| **Input length** | does the chunk fit? |
| **Dimensions** | storage and speed |
| **Licence** | commercial use |
| **Speed** | CPU vs. GPU |
| **Quality** | a test on your own data |

A benchmark score is only indicative. A test in your own domain decides.

## Testing

1. Take **20 questions** whose answers you know.
2. For each, check whether the right chunk is in the top 3–5.
3. If not, try a different model or different chunks.
4. Write the results down and compare.
5. Only then deal with the database and production.

## Hybrid search

Vector search is good at meaning and weaker on exact codes and numbers.
That is why it is often combined with **full-text search**:

- **vectors** — "notice period" also finds "the contract may be
  terminated",
- **full text** — finds an exact company ID, contract number or code,
- **a reranker** — reorders the results by relevance.

The hybrid is safer for documents full of numbers and abbreviations.

## Language specifics

- **Diacritics** — the model should preserve them.
- **Inflection** — the question and the document may use different
  forms of a word.
- **Compound words** — some languages build long expressions.
- **Mixed languages** — some documents are part local, part English.
- **Abbreviations** — company IDs, VAT numbers and tax codes; test them
  separately.

## Example: a question

A user asks "can the supplier end the contract with immediate effect?".
Vector search finds the article that talks about "immediate
withdrawal". Full-text search would look for "termination" and might
miss it. Combining both approaches is safer.

## How much space the index takes

Vectors are not large, but across a big archive it adds up:

```
size ≈ number of chunks × dimensions × bytes per number
```

| Dimensions | 1,000 chunks | 50,000 chunks |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 768 (float32) | ~3 MB | ~154 MB |
| 1,024 (float32) | ~4 MB | ~205 MB |
| 1,536 (float32) | ~6 MB | ~307 MB |

This is a **calculation** at 4 bytes per number. In practice float16 or
binary vectors are used, which take less. Texts and metadata are
usually larger than the vectors themselves.

## How to test the model choice

1. Prepare **20–30 questions** from real operations.
2. For each, write down which document and chunk is the correct answer.
3. Go through the top 5 results and mark whether the right chunk is
   there.
4. Compute **Recall@5** — the share of questions where it was.
5. Repeat with another model or another chunking.

Without this set you are deciding by impression. When Recall@5 comes out
high and the answers are still bad, the problem is not the embedding but
the model or the [chunking](/topics/rag/chunking/).

## When to regenerate the index

- **A change of embedding model.**
- **A change of chunking.**
- **A change of a document.**
- **A change of metadata or permissions.**
- **A change of the language version.**

The index is not the source of truth. The source documents are.

## Where the limit is

- **The model does not understand the domain** — test on your own data.
- **The chunk is too large or too small** — see
  [chunking](/topics/rag/chunking/).
- **Exact codes** — vectors are not ideal for them.
- **Freshness** — an old index returns old answers.
- **Permissions** — the model does not handle who may see what.
