**Chunking** is the splitting of a document into smaller parts that are
stored in a vector database. It is the most influential and most often
underestimated decision in RAG. A bad chunk loses the connection
between ideas; an oversized chunk floods the context.

## Short answer

- **A chunk is the unit of search**, not the unit of the model.
- **Smaller chunks** match more precisely but lose context.
- **Larger chunks** keep the thread but eat context and rank worse.
- **Split by structure** (headings, paragraphs, articles) rather than
  by a fixed character count.

## How big a chunk

| Size | Advantage | Disadvantage |
|---|---|---|
| **small** (1–2 paragraphs) | precise search | loss of context |
| **medium** (a section) | a compromise | depends on the document |
| **large** (a chapter) | keeps the thread | worse ranking, more expensive |

There is no universal number. Contracts suit articles, manuals suit
sections, e-mail suits individual messages. Test on your own questions.

## Overlap

Overlap means neighbouring chunks share some text. It helps when the
answer sits on a boundary. The downside is a larger index and duplicate
results. For most European languages a small overlap is enough; for
tables, rather none at all.

## Splitting by structure

- **Headings** — the chunk respects the chapter.
- **Paragraphs** — a natural boundary.
- **Contract articles** — numbered sections.
- **Tables** — a separate chunk with its header.
- **Lists** — kept together where possible.

A naive split every 1,000 characters can cut a sentence or a table in
half. Document structure is a better signal than length.

## Metadata

Add to every chunk:

- **document** and **page**,
- **section** or **article**,
- **date** and **version**,
- **department** and **permissions**,
- **the document hash**.

Without metadata you can neither filter nor cite. "It was found" is not
enough; you have to know where it came from.

## Language specifics

- **Diacritics** — the chunk must keep accents and other marks.
- **Inflection** — the searched term may appear in a different form.
- **Long sentences** — splitting mid-sentence hurts.
- **Tables** — local number and date formats.
- **Abbreviations** — company registration numbers, VAT IDs and tax
  codes must stay in the same chunk.

## Example: a contract

A contract has articles 1–12. A chunk per article beats a chunk per
1,000 characters: each article holds together and the metadata carries
the article number. For a price table, create a separate chunk with the
header so the columns do not fall apart.

## How to evaluate chunking

1. Take **20 questions** whose answers you know.
2. Check whether the correct chunk appeared in the **top 3–5**.
3. If not, look at whether the chunk was too small or too large.
4. Try a different split and compare.
5. Write the result down; without a baseline, changes cannot be judged.

## How many chunks a document produces

The number of chunks can be estimated before indexing:

```
count ≈ document length / (chunk size − overlap)
```

Example: a 100-page PDF at 2,000 characters per page is roughly
200,000 characters. With a chunk of 1,500 characters and an overlap of
150 characters that works out to **about 148 chunks**. This is a
**calculation**, not a measurement; the real count depends on splitting
by structure.

Why it matters: the number of chunks sets the index size, the cost of
search and how many candidates fit into the context. At 10,000
documents, the difference between 1,500 and 3,000 characters already
means thousands of extra chunks.

## Choosing by document type

| Document | Split by | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Contract | articles | numbering and paragraphs |
| Manual | sections and subsections | steps follow each other |
| E-mail | individual messages | conversation by thread |
| Table | sheet or block with a header | keeps the columns |
| Web page | H2/H3 headings | page structure |
| Meeting notes | paragraphs and decisions | topics alternate |

When a document has no structure, only then split by length — and with
an overlap that keeps a sentence whole.

## How much context the chunks take

RAG does not send the whole document, but a handful of chunks. An
indicative **calculation**: 5 chunks of 1,500 characters is 7,500
characters. At roughly 3.5 characters per token (the figure for Czech,
which is denser than English) that is around **2,100 tokens** for the
context alone. On top of that come the system prompt, the question and
the history.

The trade-off follows: more chunks improve the chance that the answer
is in the context, but they lengthen
[prefill](/dictionary/performance/prefill/) and take space in the
[KV cache](/dictionary/memory/kv-cache/). A typical operating point is
3–8 chunks, not 50.

## Common mistakes

- **Fixed length regardless of structure.**
- **Chunks that are too small** — the model loses the thread.
- **Chunks that are too large** — the context is flooded.
- **No metadata** — no citations and no filtering.
- **Duplicates** — the same text in ten chunks.
- **Tables cut** into meaningless pieces.
