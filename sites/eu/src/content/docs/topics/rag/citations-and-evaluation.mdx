RAG without citations is just a chat pretending to know your documents.
Citations are the proof that the answer comes from your data, and at the
same time the tool that exposes a hallucination. Evaluation then tells
you whether RAG works as a whole, not just whether the model can write
well.

## Short answer

- **Every answer should have a source** — document, page or section.
- **The citation is generated from metadata**, not by the model.
- **Evaluate retrieval and the answer separately.** A bad retrieval
  cannot be saved by a good model.
- **Without a rubric and a baseline, quality cannot improve.**

## Citations

For every chunk, store:

- **document** and **page**,
- **section** or **article**,
- **date** and **version**,
- **the document hash**.

The answer then cites:

> According to contract no. 123, article 5, page 3: the notice period
> is two months.

A model can get a citation wrong. That is why the **citation is
assembled from metadata** and the model only chooses which chunk it
refers to. When the model invents a page, that is an error, not
"creativity".

## Retrieval metrics

| Metric | What it says | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Recall@k** | was the right chunk in the top k? | the basic search test |
| **Precision@k** | how many of the top k were relevant? | ranking quality |
| **MRR** | how high was the first correct result? | ordering |
| **Hit rate** | was it found at all? | a quick test |

In practice **Recall@5** plus a manual check is often enough. Numbers
without context do not say whether RAG is usable.

## Evaluating the answer

| Criterion | What to watch |
|---|---|
| **Faithfulness** | the answer matches the cited chunk |
| **Completeness** | nothing important is missing |
| **Citation** | it points to the right document and page |
| **Numbers** | amounts, dates and codes are correct |
| **Language** | grammar and terminology |
| **Refusal** | when the answer is not in the data, the model says so |

The model should be able to say **"this is not in the documents"**. When
it cannot, RAG is not safe.

## Example: an answer with a citation

> According to contract no. 123, article 5, page 3, the notice period is
> two months. The document does not mention an option of immediate
> termination.

That answer is useful because it admits what the document does not say.
If the model instead invents article 9, that is a citation error.

## Rubric

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Faithfulness | invented | partial | exact |
| Citation | missing | wrong | correct |
| Completeness | missing | partial | everything |
| Numbers | error | uncertain | correct |
| Language | errors | minor | clean |

A maximum of 10 points. For production, set a threshold below which an
answer does not go out without review.

## Language specifics

- **Diacritics** in citations and names.
- **Inflection** — the citation has to be exact.
- **Numbers** — local formatting.
- **Abbreviations** — company IDs, VAT numbers and tax codes.
- **Long documents** — the model may cite the wrong page.

## How to enforce citations

It is not about asking the model nicely, but about fixing the format.
Two routes:

- **Structured output** — the answer has an `answer` field and a
  `citations` field, where each citation points to a chunk ID. The model
  cannot return free text.
- **Post-processing** — the application takes the chunk IDs that were
  actually used and fills in the document, page and date from metadata.

The second route is safer: even if the model gives a wrong ID, the
application sees that it does not exist and refuses the answer. Details
are under [structured
output](/dictionary/software/structured-output/).

## How many answers to check

A rubric is useful only when answers are actually scored with it. A
practical routine for every change:

1. **20–30 answers** to questions from operations, not invented ones.
2. **All refusals** — the model must be able to say "not in the data".
3. **All answers containing numbers** — amounts, dates, company IDs.
4. **One user without permission** — they must not receive someone
   else's document.

When scores between versions differ by one point, that is noise. Decide
on repeated measurement, not on a single impression.

## Common mistakes

- **Citing only the document**, not the page.
- **The model invents citations.**
- **A chunk without metadata.**
- **Evaluation by impression only.**
- **Testing on the same data used for development.**
- **No baseline** — you cannot tell what improved.
